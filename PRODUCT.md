# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Catholic Fasting for Android serves Catholics who want a clear, pastoral answer about fasting, abstinence, Friday penance, reminders, and seasonal discipline on a phone they already use every day. The primary context is practical and time-bound: checking what is asked today, planning the next required day, starting or ending an intermittent fast, reviewing guidance, or confirming why a rule applies.

Primary users are lay Catholics planning personal observance, people preparing for fasting and abstinence days, and premium subscribers who want steadier formation through seasonal planning, reflection, recovery, and reminders. The Android beta audience also includes testers validating billing, notification, widget, shortcut, and Play delivery behavior before public release.

## Product Purpose

The app helps users understand what the Church asks today, plan ahead for Catholic fasting and abstinence days, and build a steady rhythm of personal discipline through the liturgical year. It combines a Today dashboard, fasting-day calendar, rule guidance, citations, intermittent fast tracking, reminder center, premium formation tools, widgets, shortcuts, deep links, Play subscriptions, and local-first storage.

Success means a user can open the app and quickly answer "What should I do today?", understand why, and act with peace rather than confusion. Premium success means the app becomes a calm companion for preparation, recovery, reflection, and seasonal growth rather than a louder productivity layer.

Android remains a native Android product. The iOS app is the behavioral reference for parity-sensitive logic and product intent, but Android should use Material, Compose, Android notifications, widgets, shortcuts, deep links, and Play Billing on their own terms.

## Brand Personality

The voice is calm, reverent, practical, and transparent. It should feel like a trusted parish handout refined into a native Android app: clear enough for quick decisions, warm enough for devotional use, and careful enough around pastoral nuance.

Three-word personality: calm, reverent, practical.

## Anti-references

- Do not make the app feel like a generic wellness, fasting, diet, biohacking, or productivity tracker.
- Do not use energetic streak pressure, shame-heavy completion language, aggressive gamification, or copy that implies spiritual performance.
- Do not obscure rule uncertainty, local variation, medical prudence, or pastoral authority behind oversimplified answers.
- Do not claim public release status, current store availability, ratings, competitor standing, or benchmark scores without checking a current source.
- Do not turn sacred imagery into decoration for decoration's sake.
- Do not copy iOS visual treatments literally when Android has a better native pattern.
- Do not introduce backup, export, import, household-share, account, cloud sync, analytics, or network assumptions into the current local-only Android beta scope.
- Do not market or depend on support-tip products while the Android Play catalog excludes them.

## Design Principles

1. Daily clarity before ornament. The first job of every surface is to answer the user's immediate obligation, live fast state, or next faithful action.
2. Reverence through restraint. Sacred imagery, seasonal color, and serif moments should support prayerful attention, not compete with the task.
3. Transparent authority. Rules, citations, regional profiles, local variation, and rationale should remain close enough that users can inspect the source of guidance.
4. Android-native calm. Navigation, controls, cards, chips, notifications, widgets, shortcuts, and billing should feel native to Android rather than mirrored from another platform.
5. Formation without pressure. Tracking, reminders, premium tools, and recovery flows should encourage steady discipline without shame or performative metrics.
6. Truth before polish. Product claims, release statements, store copy, health scope, and parity claims must match the shipped Android app and current release docs.

## Accessibility And Inclusion

The product should meet or exceed Android accessibility expectations for phone-first Compose UI. Preserve stable semantics and identifiers where tests depend on them, support readable text wrapping, avoid color-only meaning, keep touch targets large enough for repeated daily use, and keep dense controls understandable with screen readers.

Motion should remain subtle and purposeful, with system reduced-motion expectations respected where applicable. Copy should support English and Spanish release surfaces, keep French Canadian content where already implemented, avoid idioms that are hard to translate, and stay clear about medical prudence, pastoral advice, and local diocesan authority.
